How to read this

Flagged is the share of a group's claims sent for review. Actually fraud is the share that really were fraud in the data, so you can see whether a higher flag rate matches a higher real fraud rate. Honest claims wrongly flagged is the most important column for fairness: it shows who gets pulled into review without cause.

Not-flagged ratio compares the group least often left alone with the group most often left alone. The "80% rule" is a screening rule of thumb, not a legal test, and it is noisy for groups of a few hundred claims. A value below 0.80 is a reason to investigate, not proof of bias.

Limitations: the training data is a public synthetic dataset of 1,000 claims, so these results show the audit method working, not how a real insurer's model would behave. Every flagged claim goes to a human reviewer; nothing is rejected automatically.
